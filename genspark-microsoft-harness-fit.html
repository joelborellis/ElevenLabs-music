Making sense of AI agents

Genspark is a harness. Microsoft is the foundation.

AI agents are multiplying, and it's getting hard to tell them apart. This is a simple way to think about them — the idea of a "harness" — and why pairing a capable harness like Genspark with a trusted foundation like Microsoft is such a strong fit. Including the honest places where one side gains more than the other.

Start here

What's a harness?

An AI model — the kind built by OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google — is like an engine. Powerful, but on its own it doesn't actually do anything for you.

A harness is the car built around that engine — the wheel, the dashboard, the connection to the road. It turns raw intelligence into something a person can actually drive to get work done.

In plain terms, the harness is the part knowledge workers actually open and use. It connects the AI to your real tools, keeps track of what you're working on, and gets things done on your behalf. Genspark is a harness. Microsoft Copilot is a harness. The coding tools your engineers use — Claude Code, Codex, GitHub Copilot — are harnesses too. They share the same shape — and there will always be more than one, because different jobs call for different harnesses.

The job it does

A harness does three things a raw model can't.

01

Connects

It links the AI to your real world — your email, your files, the web — so it's working with actual information, not guessing.

02

Remembers

It keeps track of what you're working on, so each request builds on the last instead of starting from a blank page.

03

Acts

It doesn't just answer questions — it does the work and hands you something finished: a draft, a deck, a piece of research.

The example

Genspark is a harness — and a strong one.

Genspark calls itself an "AI super agent." In our terms, that's a particularly capable harness. Rather than relying on a single AI model, it combines several of the best — ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini among them — and pairs them with dozens of tools.

The result is finished work, not just answers: research pulled together, documents written, presentations built. That's a harness doing its job unusually well — which is exactly why it's worth understanding how it fits into a company.

Two ways to use it

From Genspark's app, or from inside Microsoft — both run on Microsoft.

There are two ways people reach Genspark. They feel different from the outside, but underneath they lean on the same place.

Way 1From Genspark's app

Straight from Genspark's own app

An employee signs in at Genspark — on the website or its installable app (a Progressive Web App that sits on the desktop like any other program) — and connects their work account. Fast and self-serve, but no one in the company is watching.

  • It still reaches into your Microsoft information to do the work
  • It still runs on Microsoft's cloud underneath
  • But IT can't see it, and there are no controls
Way 2From inside Microsoft

Right inside Teams and the Microsoft apps

IT deploys Genspark so it shows up inside the tools people already use — recognized, supervised, and properly governed.

  • The same Microsoft information, but permission-checked
  • The same Microsoft cloud, now with security and oversight
  • Leadership can see it, steer it, and switch it off
▾      ▾
The point worth noticing

Either way, Microsoft is underneath — it's your data and your cloud doing the work. You never really use Genspark without using Microsoft too.

So the real choice isn't "Microsoft or Genspark." It's whether Genspark runs on Microsoft with oversight, or without it. And that's exactly the case for bringing it in the front door.

Why it's a good fit

Each brings what the other doesn't.

Neither company is trying to be the other. That's exactly what makes the pairing work.

Microsoft brings

The trusted foundation

  • Security, identity, and oversight a large company requires
  • The cloud everything runs on
  • One safe, organized view of the company's own knowledge
  • The platform leadership already trusts
Genspark brings

A uniquely capable agent

  • The best of several AI models, not just one
  • Dozens of tools that produce finished work
  • A polished experience people actually want to use
  • Specialized capability Microsoft doesn't have to build

A great agent, on a foundation you trust.

The honest part

The value doesn't split evenly — and that's fine.

In any good partnership, the benefits aren't perfectly equal in every place. It's worth naming plainly, because it's a sign of a healthy fit, not a problem to solve.

Microsoft gains

A stronger platform

Genspark runs on Microsoft's security, cloud, and even much of its AI. Every capable agent that joins makes Microsoft's foundation more valuable.

Genspark gains

Reach and trust

It reaches large enterprises it couldn't on its own — and inherits instant credibility, because Microsoft's oversight vouches for it.

The customer gains

Power, used safely

A genuinely powerful agent that can be adopted with confidence, because it's governed rather than slipping in the side door.

The takeaway on balance

Three winners, uneven in places. That's how strong platform partnerships are supposed to work.

In a sentence
Don't fear the flood of AI agents. See the one thing they share — they're all harnesses — then welcome the capable ones, and run them on a foundation you trust.

Appendix — for the technical reader

How access and billing actually work.

The page above is the plain-language version. This is the mechanics, at a glance — what authenticates, where the request goes, and who meters it — for each of the two doors. The short version: in both, the prompt and the data leave Microsoft for Genspark's cloud; what changes is the identity model and the governance around it.

01  Request & authentication
Door 1 — from Genspark's own app
User→ Genspark app / PWA→ Entra OAuth consent→ Genspark backend (Azure)→ MS Graph→ Mailbox→ Result
IdentityMulti-tenant app → a service principal (enterprise app) in your tenant
TokenOAuth 2.0 auth-code + PKCE; delegated, per-user scopes (Mail.Read, Calendars.Read, …)
UsedServer-side, from Genspark's cloud — the token calls Graph on the user's behalf
Door 2 — from inside Teams (Agent 365)
User in Teams→ Genspark bot endpoint→ Genspark agent runtime (Azure)→ MS Graph (OBO token)→ Mailbox→ Answer in Teams
IdentityA first-class Entra Agent ID — the agent is a governed citizen of the tenant
TokenOn-behalf-of (OBO) the signed-in user; single sign-on, no separate Genspark login to function
GovernedConditional Access · Purview DLP · Defender · audit trail · admin off-switch
The tell, true of both doors: the orchestration and reasoning run in Genspark's cloud (confirmed Azure), so the prompt and the retrieved mailbox data leave the Microsoft boundary either way. Door 2 doesn't keep the data in-boundary — it trims it to permissions, runs it on-behalf-of the user, and logs it. To verify in your own tenant: Entra ID → Enterprise applications → Genspark → Permissions (delegated vs application) and Sign-in logs.
02  Billing — who meters what
Door 1 — Genspark's app
Two parties, light
Microsoft side
Nothing extra. Rides on the M365 seats users already hold; Graph access is free.
Genspark side
Free tier (100 Genspark credits/day per user) or a direct Genspark subscription.
Door 2 — Teams / Agent 365
Two parties, metered
Microsoft side
Agent 365 per-user license (~$15, or bundled in E7)  +  Work IQ → Copilot Credits if it grounds through Work IQ. Admin must enable consumptive billing; credits are ~$0.01 each, via prepaid packs or pay-as-you-go to an Azure subscription.
Genspark side
Free tier (100 Genspark credits/day) or the Genspark Enterprise plan + DPA for full access — direct or via Marketplace.
Don't conflate the meters. Genspark credits ≠ Microsoft Copilot Credits — they're separate currencies on separate bills. Through Door 2, up to three meters can run at once: the Agent 365 seat, Microsoft Copilot Credits, and Genspark's own credits.
